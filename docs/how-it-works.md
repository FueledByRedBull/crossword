# How the generator works

The rules the pipeline applies, stage by stage. The README has the commands; the measurements behind the choices are in [evaluation.md](evaluation.md); the design and the backlog are in [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md).

## Pipeline

1. Score the articles linked from the seed and choose how many to keep (`K`). A linked article is on topic when its intro uses a word of the seed title (matched by stem; a Greek word in -ία also matches its -ος and -ικός forms, "φιλόσοφος" for "Φιλοσοφία") or the seed article names it at least twice. Where the word-frequency list tells the title's words apart, only the rarest counts ("Rome", not "ancient", in "Ancient Rome"), and a derived form of a short word ("Roman") counts only in the intro's first sentence. The list is `data/lexicon/combined_wordfreq.txt`, built by `scripts/build_combined_lexicon.py` from `scowl_wordfreq.txt` (SCOWL frequency tiers) and `filler_words.txt` (a Hunspell word list; the words SCOWL lacks enter at the lowest score; where the lists come from is in `data/lexicon/README.md`). For an article whose intro is off topic the seed has to name the page's own title, not a redirect to it ("axiomatic" does not name "Axiom"), and a proper name counts only where it stands alone with a capital letter ("the wave nature of light" does not name the journal "Nature", "Camille Pissarro" does not name the film "Camille"). Articles whose intro is on topic are selected first, then those the seed merely names. Off-topic articles are cut (`cut_off_topic` in `candidate_scores.csv`) and are selected only when nothing else is left. An on-topic article is never cut. How close its text is to the seed's (a TF-IDF cosine) orders the articles and, with how often the seed names a page, decides which titles the answer pool keeps when more than 150 are on topic; a seed with fewer keeps them all. That closeness depends on how long and how broad the seed article is, so no fixed value of it is a fair threshold. Pages on sexual content or on sexual abuse and exploitation are kept out of a puzzle: a page whose title or first sentence uses a word of a short list per language (`src/text_normalize.py`) is cut as `cut_sensitive` and can never be selected, and an answer or a clue text that uses one is dropped. A seed whose own title uses one turns the filter off for that run. War, slavery, genocide, execution, death and disease are not on the list. A word list cannot see a page that avoids its words, and it blocks a page that merely holds one.
2. Extract answers primarily from the titles of the on-topic linked articles, then from terms in the selected articles. A page about a person is answered by the surname (3 to 15 letters): `MONET` for "Claude Monet", `KELVIN` for "Lord Kelvin", `HEISENBERG` for "Werner Heisenberg". The title stays whole when the surname follows a lower-case word or a name particle ("Vincent van Gogh", "Cato the Younger", "Giuseppe De Nittis"); when it is a word of the seed title; when it is another page's title, unless the seed uses it alone at least twice; when several people share it and the seed does not name one of them in full more often than all the others together; and when the person's own page, after its first sentence, calls them more often by another word of the title ("Ding Liren"). A person the page calls more often by the last two words of the title than by the surname or any other word has a double surname and is answered by both: `SALAZARALLEN` (shown as 7,5) for "Noris Salazar Allen", `MAYNARDSMITH` for "John Maynard Smith". When the two together are no answer (over 15 letters) the title stays whole. The caption of the page's infobox counts as such a use ("Salazar Allen in 2017", on a page whose sentences only say "She"); it is read from the lead wikitext that scoring has already cached. As long as no text uses the surname alone, the title also stays whole when the lead writes the name in Chinese, Japanese or Korean script (the family name may come first) or gives a birth name that shares no word with the title ("Man Ray", born Emmanuel Radnitzky). A page is about a person when the parenthesis that opens its lead says "born" or "fl." or gives years that span a life (15 to 110 years: a war or an era is no person); a Greek lead that opens on "Ο" or "Η" is also a person's when it says "γεννήθηκε", "γεννημένος", "γεννημένη" or "γενν.", or "γεν." before a date. Any other page is answered by the last word of its title only when the seed uses that word as a name at least twice (`CARTHAGE` for "Ancient Carthage"), and a word that ends the titles of two or more such pages ("Empire") is nobody's name. In Greek only people are named this way, and only in the title's own written form ("Κέπλερ"). A selected article that is a redirect ("Αυτί") gives no second answer for the page it leads to when that page's title already is one ("Αφτί"). A vocabulary gate checks there are enough answers. When there are not, a rescue ladder loosens the term filters and then widens the pool, first with pages that link to the seed (at most 300; those whose first sentence uses a seed word rank first and are the ones selected and used as clue sources; the others that use it in a later sentence can still be answers, unless the only use is another form of the word that is an adverb, as in "θερμοδυναμικά σταθερή") and then with pages linked from the on-topic selected articles (at most 100 new pages from at most 6 articles). If that still leaves fewer clued answers than `--gate-min` but at least `--min-words`, the run goes on with the thin pool (`accept_thin_pool` in `diagnostics_rescue.json`).
3. Build one clue per answer: a definition clue from the article's first sentence (answer masked), or a fill-in-the-blank clue as fallback. A lead whose main verb is not "is" gives a definition that opens on "It" or "They" ("It reflects the average kinetic energy of the particles in a system"). When a person's first sentence gives only nationality and occupation, it is joined with what the second or third sentence says the person did ("An English physicist who studied the nature of heat and discovered its relationship to mechanical work"). A person whose first sentence gives no definition is tried again with a head of two words or more ("Γάλλος αστρονόμος, γνωστός κυρίως ..."; Greek has no indefinite article, so "Ολλανδός φυσικός" is the whole of "A Dutch physicist"). The head is joined the same way or shown with the given names ("Ολλανδός φυσικός (Χέντρικ ____)") and is never a clue by itself. For a page that is no person, a cut that stops right before a relative clause gives way to the longer cut through it ("A type of male singing voice whose vocal range lies between the countertenor and baritone voice types"); when there is no such cut and the genus has fewer than three content words ("A young child", "Η επιστήμη και η τέχνη"), it comes after the fill-ins. A fill-in comes only from the seed article, the answer's own page or a selected article whose intro is on topic. The seed and the selected articles are read in full, up to their first reference section ("See also", "References", "Works", "Selected publications" and the like, in English and Greek), so a line of a bibliography is never a clue. Whole sentences come before cut ones and the seed's before the others; among the rest the sentence wins that repeats the most of the intro of the answer's own page, each shared word counted by how rare it is among the pool's lead sentences ("A ____ machine is a hypothetical machine that can do work indefinitely without an external energy source" rather than "As a result, there is no possibility of a ____ machine"). For a person's name the sentence must write the name with a capital wherever it occurs ("kelvin" the unit does not clue Kelvin). A page defines only its own name: its title, or a name of the person it is about ("Monet", "Peter Tait" for "Peter Guthrie Tait"). The name of a redirect to another kind of page ("Subatomic" for "Subatomic particle") takes fill-ins only, and a short name of a page that is not about a person ("Carthage") is clued only by seed sentences where the name stands alone. A page that may be another sense of the seed's word (a disambiguated title, or an intro that opens by sending the reader to another article, with an off-topic intro) gets no definition and its own text clues nothing: only the seed or an on-topic selected article can clue it. When the first sentence gives no usable definition and no fill-in exists, the second sentence of the intro is tried ("He was one of the most influential figures in the development of jazz piano."). A person answered by the surname whose definition is generic only for want of informative words ("An American jazz drummer"), and whom nothing else clues, gets that definition with the given names of the page title and a blank for the surname: "An American jazz drummer (Art ____)". It comes last, after the fill-ins and the second-sentence definition, and is not used when it would pass 18 words.
4. Place the clued answers in a free-form layout, where words cross wherever their letters match. The search is a small Rust program (`layout-rs`) that fills the grid greedily, then keeps removing a few words and refilling (simulated annealing), about 72,000 steps by default; for the same answers and `--random-seed` it gives the same layout on every platform, because it uses only exact arithmetic (its own random numbers and its own logarithm, no math library). The answers and their scores come from the stages before it, which use the platform's math library and the language model, so a whole run is only sure to repeat on one machine. People fill at most a third of the answers offered to the layout, unless too few other answers have a clue: short surnames place easily and would otherwise crowd out works, styles and places.
5. Package the puzzle with attribution.

## Layout rules

A layout is valid only if:

- every word crosses at least one other word;
- all words form one connected piece;
- every run of two or more letters in a row or column is exactly one placed word.

`fill_status` is `complete` when the layout is valid and has at least `--min-words` words, otherwise `failed`. Layout quality is reported as `word_count`, `across_count`, `down_count`, `crossing_count`, `mean_crossings_per_word`, `words_with_two_plus_crossings`, `two_plus_crossings_ratio`, `letter_count`, `checked_letter_ratio`, `bbox_rows`, `bbox_cols` and `density`. `--layout-candidates` caps how many clued answers are offered to the layout and `--layout-restarts` scales the search effort (750 search steps each).

## Clue quality

- Each entry has `clue_style` of `definition` or `fill_in_blank`; a fill-in-the-blank clue must contain the blank (`____`). A definition that names a person's given names ("An American jazz drummer (Art ____)") has one too and counts as a `definition`.
- A clue must not contain the answer, a word of it or a morphological cousin of one ("arts" does not clue "French art"). A spelling that differs only by accents, a modifier letter or an apostrophe is the same word ("Hawaiʻi, sometimes written ____" does not clue Hawaii).
- A clue has at most 18 words. A definition is cut only at a clean boundary: never past a colon or inside a name, and inside a relative clause only before a time phrase that follows the clause's object ("... who formed the Second Triumvirate alongside Octavian and Mark Antony", cut before "during"), and never after a bare "the years" or "the period" that the sentence goes on to complete ("... a pioneering band leader in New Orleans [in the years from ...]"). An example phrase in mid-sentence is left out when the cut would stop inside it ("A term used by Post-Impressionist artists [like Paul Gauguin, Émile Bernard and Louis Anquetin] to distinguish their work stylistically from Impressionism"). A cut may keep exactly the limit, and a relative clause may also end before a preposition whose object is a date ("... that studies the growth, reproduction and evolution of plants [over the last 3.5 billion years]"). A noun is not cut from the preposition it wants (interest, figure, role, conjunction, cooperation, border, relationships, συνεισφορά, συμβολή); a comma tail that opens on "with" or a verb is dropped whole when the cut would leave its last noun unfinished ("..., with a libretto [by ...]", needs the parser); and a cut that ends where no noun phrase ends ("... illustrator best", "a title he") leaves the next shorter clean cut its chance. Two names with commas are a place ("New Orleans, Louisiana,") only when no third follows ("Judea, Idumea, Peraea, ..."). The copula of a relative clause or of an aside ("tensions that are accumulated ...", "όπως είναι ευρέως γνωστός") is not the copula of the sentence, and the subject may carry a lower-case prefix hyphenated to the answer ("αλ-Χουαρίζμι") but not a capitalised compound ("Neo-Impressionism"). A fill-in-the-blank clue is a whole sentence, or the start of one cut at a clause boundary (`...` at the end only) that still has its finite verb.
- A fill-in-the-blank clue never opens with a word that points at an earlier sentence ("It", "This", "But", "Hence," ...) and does not lean on one further in: a pronoun with nothing before it to refer to ("In 1870 he introduced ..."), "this pump" when no pump was named, "the former", "the following", "doing so", "as noted above", and "the term" or "the name" with nothing in the sentence saying which ("Originally, the term was confined to ..."), and a "his" or "her" that only a pronoun subject explains ("On his 1961 tour of ____, he was booed ...", needs the parser). In Greek, which drops its subject, a sentence that opens on a verb with no subject of its own and a possessive ("της") with nothing before it to refer to are rejected as well (both need the parser).
- A definition is dropped as generic when it has no word that is rare among the pool's lead sentences (words of the seed title and nationalities do not count); when it opens on the answer's nationality and has fewer than two such words ("An American jazz drummer"); when its head noun names a kind of word without saying what the word means ("A term coined in the 1980s"); or when it opens on a vague quantifier ("Any of many different quantities") or is as empty as one ("A separate part of a larger system"). A nationality on a kind-of-word head noun names a language, not the answer's nationality ("The common English title for a series of impressionist paintings by Claude Monet" is kept).
- A blank that is one part of a longer proper name is not a clue.
- A blank that is one item of a list of three or more ("Egypt, ____ and Tunisia") is not a clue: any other item would fit as well. A pair ("Monet and ____") stays.
- Clue text must not repeat across entries.
- Every used clue carries provenance (article, revision id, sentence offset, `oldid` URL). Clue text and revision id always come from the same API response: the full text for the seed and the selected articles, the intro for every other page. A definition joined from two sentences of a page cites the later one. A clue without such a revision id is not used.

## Greek

`--lang el` reads el.wikipedia.org. Answers are Greek capital letters without accents (Θερμότητα becomes `ΘΕΡΜΟΤΗΤΑ`); titles with letters of another alphabet are not answers. Definitions are cut from Greek lead sentences (`είναι`, `ήταν`, `ονομάζεται` ...), and the count of how often the seed names a page tolerates inflection endings.

Only a person is answered by a short name in Greek, in the title's own written form ("Κέπλερ" for "Γιοχάνες Κέπλερ"): other names inflect. The Greek tagger often calls a masculine noun a preposition, an adverb or a verb ("Γάλλος", "συγγραφέας", "γλύπτης"), which used to reject sound definitions; at the two ends of a definition a word tagged as a preposition that is none, and one tagged as an adverb or a verb that ends in -ος or -ης and is no participle, therefore counts as a noun. Known gaps: the "It ..." definition and the joined definition for people are English only, the check for a generic definition is weaker in Greek than in English (no nationality or kind-of-word rule), there is no Greek word-frequency list (the lexicon score is 0), a fill-in-the-blank clue only blanks the exact written form of the answer, a definition of two words ("Γάλλος φιλόσοφος") is under the three-word minimum and a long first sentence with no clean cut inside 16 words gives none, and el.wikipedia.org rate-limits sooner than en.wikipedia.org, so a first online run waits on HTTP 429 a few times.
